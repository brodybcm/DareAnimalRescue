Adoption Overview

How To Adopt From D.A.R.E.

Our thorough adoption process is designed to ensure a safe, lifelong match between your home and our rescues.

1

Submit Application

Complete our online Adoption Application with details on your household, schedule, and living arrangements.

2

Veterinary Reference Check

All existing dogs and cats in the household must be spayed/neutered, up to date on core vaccines, and on monthly heartworm prevention.

3

Landlord / HOA Consent

If renting or living in an HOA community, we verify pet permission, breed allowances, and weight limits with your property manager.

4

Phone Interview

A brief, friendly phone chat with an adoption coordinator to discuss the pet’s personality, energy level, and your lifestyle.

5

Meet & Greet

Meet the pet in person at their foster home or an adoption event. Introduce family members and existing pets.

6

2-Week Foster-to-Adopt Trial

Bring the pet home on trial for two weeks to ensure a smooth transition with zero pressure before making the commitment final.

Community Cat Program

TNR: Trap-Neuter-Return

Trap-Neuter-Return (TNR) is the only proven, humane, and effective method for managing feral and community cat colonies in Valdosta and Lowndes County. Through TNR, feral cats are humanely trapped, medically sterilized, rabies vaccinated, ear-tipped for identification, and returned to their outdoor home under caretaker supervision.

Humanely Ends Breeding

Halts the endless cycle of homeless litters born outside.

Reduces Shelter Euthanasia

Keeps unadoptable feral cats out of municipal shelter intake.

Healthier Communities

Curbing nuisance behaviors like yowling, marking, and fighting.

Have a Community Cat Colony?

If you are feeding outdoor community cats in the Lowndes County area, contact us to learn about humane trapping guidance and local low-cost TNR resources.

Owner Guidance

Rehoming Your Pet

We understand that unforeseen life changes, medical crises, and housing restrictions happen. Here is our expert guide to protecting your pet throughout the rehoming process.

1. Can the Situation Be Resolved?

Before making the heartbreaking choice to rehome, consider whether temporary assistance can help your pet stay in their home:

  • Pet-Friendly Housing: Check resources like MyPitBullIsFriendly or Zillow pet filters for rental listings without arbitrary weight or breed restrictions.
  • Behavioral Challenges: Most litter box issues, jumping, or leash reactivity can be resolved with positive reinforcement training or a quick vet check for urinary infections.
  • Temporary Medical Hardships: Contact us to see if short-term foster assistance or pet food pantry support is available.

2. Steps to Safely Rehome Your Pet Yourself

Because local rescues and shelters are constantly at maximum capacity, keeping your pet safely in your home while looking for a new adopter is the safest path:

  • Get Medical Records in Order: Ensure rabies and core vaccinations are up to date and obtain your pet’s complete veterinary printout.
  • Be 100% Honest: Always disclose quirks, medical requirements, how they react to other dogs, cats, or young kids. Transparent profiles create lasting matches.
  • Adopt-a-Pet & Petfinder Rehome: Use trusted community rehoming networks such as Adopt-a-Pet Rehome where adopters are pre-screened.

⚠️ CRITICAL SAFETY WARNING: Never Advertise "Free to Good Home"

Never post pets for free on Craigslist, Facebook Marketplace, or yard sales. Free animals are frequently targeted by dogfighting rings (as bait animals), backyard puppy mill breeders, and fraudulent resellers. Always charge a nominal rehoming fee, verify a veterinary reference, and conduct a home visit before releasing your pet.

Lost & Found Protocol

Options for Found Animals

If you have found a stray dog, cat, or litter of kittens in Valdosta or Lowndes County, follow these critical steps immediately to reunite them with their worried family.

1

Scan For Microchip (Free)

Take the animal to any local vet clinic or 24-hour emergency animal hospital in Valdosta. They will scan for a microchip free of charge to find the owner's phone number.

2

File "Found Pet" Report

Notify Lowndes County Animal Services (229-671-2760) and surrounding municipal shelters so they can match the animal against incoming lost pet claims.

3

Post on Social Media

Post clear photos and cross streets on Facebook groups: Valdosta Lost and Found Pets, Lowndes County Pets, Nextdoor, and file a free lost report on PawBoost.

4

Contact D.A.R.E. Coordinator

Email our dedicated Lost & Found coordinator with photos, location found, and your contact info so we can cross-reference our lost animal database.
